Why Consensus Standard Parts?

Using consensus standard fasteners over custom "manufactured to print" parts offers significant advantages across cost, quality, and supply chain flexibility.

💰 Cost Advantage

Standard parts are manufactured in high volumes by multiple suppliers worldwide, driving down unit cost through competition and economies of scale. Custom print parts carry tooling, setup, and minimum order premiums.

⏱ Lead Time

Standard fasteners are stocked globally. When you need 500 pieces of an M10 hex bolt, they ship today. A custom fastener may require 8–16 weeks for tooling, first article inspection, and production.

✅ Quality Assurance

Consensus standards (IFI, ISO, ASME, DIN, SAE) define dimensional tolerances, mechanical properties, and test methods. Independent certification and traceability are built in. No need to specify — the standard does it for you.

🔗 Supply Chain Flexibility

Standard parts can be sourced from dozens of manufacturers worldwide. If one supplier has a shortage or quality issue, you can qualify an alternate in days, not months.

📐 Engineering Confidence

Published standards provide documented design data: proof loads, tensile strength, torque values, and dimensional drawings. Your engineering team can rely on published data rather than generating it themselves.

♻️ Sustainability

Standardized designs reduce engineering waste and the need for custom tooling. Globally stocked parts reduce transportation distances when sourced regionally. Traceability requirements in many standards support responsible sourcing.

Guided Tour — Which Tool, and Why

This generator turns the SBS technical database into documents you can hand to a supplier, an inspector, or an engineer. Each tab answers a different question — start with the one that matches what you are trying to produce.

📄 TDS Generator

Use when you need documentation for one specific part. Identify the controlling standard, define the attributes (size, material, grade, finish), and generate a Technical Data Sheet and an Inspection Plan for that exact configuration. This is the main workflow.

🔎 Two ways to start

From a Standard — search or browse the category and standard cards when you know the specification but not the part number. From an SBS Part # — paste a part number and the tool decodes it back into its attributes and pre-fills the form. Anything the part number does not encode is flagged so you can confirm it.

✅ Inspection Plan

Generated alongside the TDS. Lists the characteristics to check, the criteria and tolerances, and the inspection method, with fields to record results and pass/fail. Add the inspector name and lot number, then download it as a PDF, or export the full Excel inspection template.

📊 Standard Data Sheet

Use for reference across a whole standard. Instead of one configured part, this shows every size in a standard as a single summary table — dimensional, mechanical, or chemical. Useful for selection and quick lookup.

⚖️ Compare Parts

Use when deciding between two options. Configure two parts side by side and see the attributes, dimensions, and properties aligned, with differences highlighted — for example the same washer in two different finishes.

📘 Best Practices

Background engineering guidance — why consensus standard parts are usually preferable to custom "manufactured to print" parts, covering cost, quality, lead time, and sustainability.

⬇️ Downloads

Every output can leave the tool. The TDS, Comparison, and Standard Data Sheet each have a Download PDF button; the Inspection Plan downloads as a watermarked PDF or as an Excel template. Add your own part number with Add Alternate Part # before downloading and it appears on the document.

🔄 Keeping data current

All values come from the SBS Technical Database and load when the page opens. If the underlying data has just been updated, use Refresh Data in the header to reload without a full page refresh.
