About This Tool

The SBS Flat Washer Reference Tool helps you work out which flat washer you are holding, find the washer that suits a joint, and compare the published standards side by side. It is built for sales, purchasing, engineering and customers who need to answer questions such as "What is this washer?", "What goes under this bolt?" and "Are these two standards interchangeable?"

It is a reference aid, not a substitute for the standard or for engineering judgement. All dimensions are the nominal values published in each standard. The tool covers 0 size entries across 12 standards, metric and inch.

How Measurement Matching Works

Calipers and micrometers are not perfect, and a real washer is rarely exactly nominal. The Identify tab allows for both. A standard is shown when your reading falls inside a window around the washer's nominal size. The window is twice the typical error of the instrument, widened by the standard's own published tolerance where this tool has it.

The error figures are SBS engineering estimates for careful use, not instrument specifications. Each result is labeled In tolerance (your reading is inside the standard's tolerance band) or Close (outside the band but inside the window), and the deviation from nominal is shown for each dimension you entered. Untick "Include standard tolerance" to use the instrument window alone. Several standards share dimensions, so a measurement alone cannot always tell them apart. Check the hardness and markings as well.

How the Bolt Grade Check and Guide Me Work

Each standard is placed on a hardness ladder using the hardness class and the bolts it is stated to suit. A washer softer than the bolt calls for will embed under load and lose clamp force, so it is flagged.

Where a standard does not state a bolt class (DIN 9021, ASME B18.22.1, ASTM F844), the tool treats it conservatively as level 1. For stainless bolts (A2-50 to A4-80, ISO 3506-1) the check looks for a stainless washer to avoid galvanic corrosion and galling. Guide Me then ranks the remaining washers by series (normal for general use, large for soft material or oversized holes, small for confined heads, hardened for structural joints) and lists the washers it ruled out and why.

Data Notes and Limitations

  • Nominal values. Dimensions are the nominal sizes in the standard. Tolerances are shown in millimetres, calculated from the standard's maximum and minimum limits.
  • Unit conversion. Metric standards are published in mm and inch standards in inches. The other unit is converted (25.4 mm per inch), so converted values can differ from a published figure in the last digit.
  • Non-preferred sizes. Sizes the standard lists as non-preferred are marked NP. They exist but are less commonly stocked.
  • ISO 7415 thickness. The standard gives thickness as a range rather than a nominal. The tool shows the maximum and applies the range as the tolerance.
  • DIN 125 A was withdrawn and replaced by ISO 7089. It is kept because many drawings still call it out.
  • Edition. Each link points to the library's current edition for that standard. The inch standards link to the withdrawn ASME B18.22.1 (1998) and to the ASTM F844 abstract. Check the standards body for the latest revision.
  • Pending data. Tolerances for the inch standards and ISO 7091 are still being added and show as pending. Additional standards will be added as data is verified.
  • Not included. Coatings and platings, spring and lock washers, and live stock or pricing. Use the search button in each detail view to find matching items in the SBS catalog.

The information below covers key engineering considerations for selecting the correct flat washer. Always verify against applicable standards and consult a qualified engineer for safety-critical applications.
Washer Sizing
Inner Diameter (ID) Selection

The ID should be slightly larger than the bolt shank to allow free assembly. Most standards specify an ID clearance of approximately 5 to 15% over the nominal bolt diameter. Oversized IDs reduce bearing area and can lead to pull-through under load.

Outer Diameter (OD) Guidelines

Select OD based on the bearing area needed to distribute the clamp load without yielding the mating surface. For soft materials (aluminum, plastics, wood), use large-series washers (ISO 7093, fender) to spread load. For steel-to-steel joints with hardened surfaces, standard OD is generally sufficient.

Hardness Selection
Why Hardness Matters

Washer hardness must be matched to the bolt grade and joint requirements. A washer that is too soft will embed under load (reducing clamp force). A washer that is too hard may damage coating or plating on mating surfaces.

Key Rules
  • Never use unhardened washers (F844, ISO 7091) under high-strength bolts (Grade 10.9 / Grade 8 and above)
  • ASTM F436 hardened washers are required under the turned element (nut or bolt head) when tightening ASTM A325 and A490 structural bolts
  • For stainless steel bolts, match washer hardness to avoid galling

Galvanic Corrosion Warning

Mixing dissimilar metals accelerates corrosion. Match washer material to bolt material where possible. When mixing is unavoidable, use an insulating washer or dielectric coating between dissimilar metals (e.g., steel bolts in aluminum structure).

Decision Checklist
  • Is the joint structural? Use hardened washers: ASTM F436 (inch) or ISO 7415 (metric, M12 to M36). For other safety-critical joints use ISO 7089 / 7090 at minimum
  • Is the substrate soft (aluminum, plastic, wood)? Use large series (ISO 7093)
  • Is space constrained under a cheese or socket head? Use ISO 7092 (small series)
  • Is the environment corrosive? Select stainless or coated washers
  • Does the standard need to match a bolt spec? Confirm HV rating compatibility
Installation and Inspection
Correct Orientation
  • Chamfered washers (ISO 7090): chamfer faces toward the bolt head or nut (turned element)
  • Place the smooth face against the mating surface to avoid damage to coatings
  • Do not stack multiple washers as a substitute for correct thickness selection
Pre-Installation Checks
  • Verify ID clears the bolt shank and thread runout
  • Confirm OD does not overlap adjacent fasteners or features
  • Check washer is flat and free of burrs or rolled edges that could affect seating
  • For structural joints, confirm F436 is required and verify heat number traceability if applicable
Reuse Policy

Hardened structural washers (F436) should generally not be reused after a full pretension cycle. Plain washers in non-structural applications may be reused if undamaged. Inspect for embedding, cracking, or deformation before reuse.

Tolerance and Fit
Product Grade A vs C

Grade A (ISO 7089, 7090, 7092, 7093-1) specifies tighter tolerances on ID, OD, and flatness, making it suitable for precision and structural applications. Grade C (ISO 7091, ISO 7093-2) has wider tolerances and is used in lower-precision or non-critical general assembly.

Tolerance Interaction with Bolt Clearance
  • The nominal ID in most standards already includes clearance over the bolt shank
  • The upper ID tolerance adds further clearance, which is acceptable for load bearing
  • The lower ID tolerance (typically 0) ensures the washer always clears the bolt shank without forcing
  • For high-vibration environments, smaller ID clearance helps maintain washer position
Thickness Tolerance Notes

Thickness tolerance affects joint preload consistency. Thicker-than-nominal washers can reduce effective bolt engagement. For precision joints, verify against the tolerance band in the applicable standard document.
